Who Is in the Trial?

This site shows who took part in clinical studies for four common conditions, and how often those studies reported participants' race. It uses records from ClinicalTrials.gov for interventional studies, which assign participants to a treatment or other intervention, that started in 2010 or later and posted results. Most records list a study phase, a stage in testing a drug. Others list no phase, such as screening, behavioral, or device studies, and the record type filter lets you include or leave them out. We don't rate any trial. Explore the numbers and draw your own conclusions.

Race of trial participants

Share of participants with known race, in trials that reported race in the standard NIH/OMB categories. Participants with unknown race are left out of the bars and shown below them. Hover over a bar to see counts.

Source: the baseline table (the table of who enrolled) of each trial in this view, from the ClinicalTrials.gov API. Uses the condition, record type, and phase filters.

Ethnicity of trial participants

Share of participants with known ethnicity, in trials that reported ethnicity in the standard NIH/OMB categories. Ethnicity is recorded separately from race, so Hispanic participants also appear in the race chart.

Source: the baseline table of each trial in this view, from the ClinicalTrials.gov API. Uses the condition, record type, and phase filters.

Average age of participants, by trial

Each trial reports one average age for its participants. Most report a mean, some report a median, and the two are combined here.

Source: the average age in the baseline table of each trial in this view, from the ClinicalTrials.gov API. Uses the condition, record type, and phase filters.

Trial participants compared with patients (PPR)

PPR is a group's share of trial participants divided by its share of people diagnosed with the condition. The dashed line marks 1.0, where the two shares are equal. The hollow marker shows the same value with the single trial that contributed the most participants to that group left out, so you can see how much one trial moves the result. It uses every record with all sites in the US, whatever the record type and phase filters are set to, so those two filters do not change it.

Sources: trial shares from the ClinicalTrials.gov API, using trials with all sites in the US. Patient shares from NCI SEER*Explorer, new cases from 2019 to 2023. Method: build_ppr.py.

How to read this

Sources and methods

  • Trial data: the ClinicalTrials.gov API, a public database of clinical studies. We include interventional studies, which assign participants to a treatment or other intervention, that started in 2010 or later and posted results. Each number comes from a study's baseline table, using the column for all participants.
  • Patient data: NCI SEER*Explorer from the National Cancer Institute, new cases of breast and prostate cancer from 2019 to 2023.
  • Full list of sources: RESEARCH.md, with the author, title, date, and link for every source we used.
  • How the numbers are made: the README explains each step, and the code on GitHub lets anyone rerun it.
