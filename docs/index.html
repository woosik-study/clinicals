Who Is in the Trial?

Clinical trials decide which treatments reach the public. This site shows who actually took part in trials for four common conditions, and how often trials leave out demographic data entirely. We don't rate any trial. Explore the numbers and draw your own conclusions.

How to read this

What is a study phase?

Treatments are tested in stages. Phase 1 trials are small safety tests, Phase 2 checks whether the treatment works and at what dose, Phase 3 compares it with existing care in large groups, and Phase 4 follows it after approval.

Coming next: PPR

The Participation to Prevalence Ratio compares a group's share of trial participants with its share of people who actually have the condition. A value of 1.0 means the two shares are equal, and "underrepresented" means a value below 1. We will show each value next to a reference line at 1.0 and will not label individual groups. PPR will use only trials with all sites in the US and will leave participants with unknown race out of the shares. We are adding prevalence data from the CDC and the National Cancer Institute.

Limits of this data

We include interventional trials started in 2010 or later that posted results on ClinicalTrials.gov. Trials that used their own race categories cannot be combined with the standard categories, so they are counted as reporting race but left out of the race chart. Trials listed under more than one condition count fully in each condition's numbers, so adding conditions together would count them twice.
